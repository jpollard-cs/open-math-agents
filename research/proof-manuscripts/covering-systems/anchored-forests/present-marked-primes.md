# When all prescribed primes must actually occur

Codex (OpenAI), November 28, 2026. Supplement to [marked-prime deletion](marked-prime-deletion.md) and [the critical window](critical-deletion.md).

In maximizing the loss from removing a collection of primes, some allowed primes could be absent. The same sharp results can also be attained with many genuinely present primes, every one of whose rows is indispensable under arbitrary reassignment. More precisely, impose the additional requirement that a prescribed finite set of marked primes between p and P, including p and P, appear in the period. Any further marked primes above or between them are still allowed. In each of the following regimes the asymptotic already stated at the smallest marked prime p holds with this extra requirement:

* For $p/T\to\alpha\in(0,1)$ take $P-p=o(p/\log T)$.
* For $\log p/\log T\to\beta\in(0,1)$ take $\log(P/p)=o(\log T)$.
* For the critical transition with $v=\log(T/p)\to\infty$ and $v^2=o((\log T)\log v)$ take $\log(P/p)=o(v)$.

For example **every prime in $[p,2p]$** can be required in the second and third regimes; the number prescribed is asymptotic to $p/\log p$. In the first regime one can prescribe every prime up to $p+p/(\log p)^2$. Their number is asymptotic to $p/(\log p)^3$ by the classical prime number theorem with its logarithmic-power error term.

**Proof.** All previous upper bounds still apply. For the construction set $T'=\lfloor pT/P\rfloor$. In each case $P\le T'$ eventually. Construct the ordinary base at $(P,T')$ by the appropriate proof, so it has period M with at least P distinct divisors through $T'$, contains no prescribed prime and has at most one highest power per prime base. All its prime bases are below p: in the fixed-ratio case they are $T^{b+o(1)}$ with $b<1$; in the other cases they are $T^{o(1)}$. Old moduli stay at most $pT$. In increasing order append each nonterminal prescribed prime q with $q-1$ rows q times different base divisors at most $T'$. Align their old tests at zero and cover the nonzero q labels. At the final prime P choose P distinct earlier divisors through $T'$, explicitly including the highest powers of all base primes and each earlier prescribed q. The number of mandatory divisors is less than P because their prime bases are all different and less than P. There are enough base divisors to complete the selection. The terminal guard lcm equals the whole preceding period. The [simultaneous prime-stage construction](../prime-budget/simultaneous.tex) then proves unrestricted irreducibility: at each ordinary or nonterminal stage every surviving prefix has a child, a stage deletion makes two, and the terminal rows can close only one old point. The aligned assignment, after deleting every marked multiple at once, leaves exactly the zero class modulo the base M.

For fixed log ratio, $(\log P)/(\log T')\to\beta$ and $\log T'\sim\log T$, so the attainable exponent is unchanged. In the transition regime $v'=\log(T'/P)=v-2\log(P/p)+o(1)\sim v$ and $\log T'\sim\log T$; formula (1) of the critical-window note has unchanged leading value. For the fixed ratio, $P-p=o(T/\log T)$ and $T-T'=o(T/\log T)$. At each fixed multiplicative shift of $y_*=y(p,T)$, replacing T by $T'$ perturbs its smooth count by at most $T-T'$. The local smooth-number increment on either side of $y_*$ has order $T/\log T$. Hence $y(P,T')/y_*\to1$, so the sharp leading constant in $-\log\Delta$ is retained as well.

The prescribed primes need not exhaust the large primes allowed by the cap, and the theorem does not claim uniform sharpness for arbitrary widely separated *mandatory* primes. A next extremal question is which wider mandatory profiles increase the necessary base period.

For a small example with both primes present, 25 distinct moduli of maximum 220 cover all integers. After removing the multiples of 7 or 11, exactly the multiples of 60 are left uncovered, of density 1/60. The same set is irreducible when all remaining residues can change after any single deletion.
