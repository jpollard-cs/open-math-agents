# A family requiring many boxes under every residue assignment

Codex (OpenAI). Companion to the [arithmetic subcover extraction](box-subcovers.md) for Erdős #1189.

There are irreducible full-period sets with $k\to\infty$ and reciprocal sum $S=(1/2+o(1))\log k$ for which **every** covering assignment requires $\Omega(\sqrt{k/\log k})$ boxes in the one-disagreement criterion. For these sets the minimum over assignments and box covers is $\Theta(\sqrt{k/\log k})$. Thus the subpower box hypothesis cannot hold for all irreducible sets, even at the known leading lower coefficient. This example has bounded surplus over its own ideal period mass. It leaves open whether a positive *normalized* surplus is possible.

## Construction

Take a zero-defect seed containing its period $P$. The ordinary rows leave one point in a chosen covering assignment after its terminal is removed. Under all assignments they leave at least one point, and deletion of another ordinary row leaves at least two. Start for instance from $2,3,4,6,12$ and apply the ordinary [prime-position extensions](linear-full-period.md) to install every prime power through $x$. Then extend the binary exponent to $\lceil2x\rceil$. Thus $P$ is divisible by all integers through $x$ and enough distinct powers of two for the dressings below. The seed has $1+B(P)$ rows. Translate the chosen terminal point to zero. Every ordinary seed row has precisely one nonzero tested digit by the zero-defect single-change count.

Split all but at most three primes of $(x,2x]$ into $t$ disjoint quartets. On each install the [previous binary diamond](cycles.md): $p-2$ unary rows for each vertex $p$, and five pair rows on the edges of two length-two paths between two terminals and their direct edge. Give the unary rows $p2^i$ distinct exponents $i=0,\ldots,p-3$, and each pair its bare prime product. All dressings concern the original seed coordinates only. Use the ordinary seed without its terminal, all the quartets, and one final full-period singleton. All moduli are distinct. In the chosen assignment, the unary rows at the old zero point remove every label except 0 and 1. Forbid $(1,0)$ on the four directed cycle edges and $(1,1)$ on the direct edge. Each quartet leaves only zero and the system covers.

For irreducibility use the binary diamond's arbitrary-assignment and deletion theorem: the ordinary rows of each quartet always leave at least one tuple, and after any row deletion at least two. This is the $d=2$ case of the [written path argument](cycles.md), with the residual edge check also in [Lean](Diamond.lean). Over an old hole only a subset of each quartet's rows can be active. Inactive rows can only increase the number of surviving tuples. The surviving tuple choices multiply because the quartets have disjoint fresh primes and dressings confined to the seed. The final singleton is therefore necessary. Deleting a seed row gives at least two old holes with surviving tuples; deleting a quartet row gives at least two surviving tuples over one old hole. Neither can be repaired by one singleton. This proves irreducibility under every reassignment after deletion.

## Lower bound independent of the covering assignment

In any covering assignment let $z$ be its full-period terminal point. The ordinary rows miss exactly $z$. Fix the old digits at $z$ and in each quartet choose two labels at each vertex: its value at $z$ and any different value surviving the active unary rows. Such a choice exists since there are at most $p-2$ unary rows, and the value at $z$ survives. Call these two values 0 and 1 locally. Each quartet in the resulting binary cube has only the all-zero survivor; otherwise a nonzero local survivor and all other quartets at zero give a second global hole.

The four points with just one local 1 must therefore be covered by four different pair rows, each testing 1 at that coordinate and 0 at its other endpoint. None covers the all-ones tuple. The fifth pair row must cover it with two tested ones, and is its only covering row on this cube. For each quartet $i$, take the global point $z_i$ with its four digits at 1 and every other digit as at $z$. Its unique covering row is that quartet's fifth pair row. The two tested ones for different $i$ belong to disjoint prime coordinates.

Any product box containing two distinct $z_i,z_j$ also contains $z$, digit by digit. On this box the full-period row must be eligible since only it covers $z$. Its reference point can therefore differ from $z$ in at most one digit. For the unique row at $z_i$ to be eligible, that reference must differ from $z$ at one of its two fresh endpoints. The same requirement for $z_j$ is disjoint. No single box in a valid cover can contain both. The $t$ points require at least $t$ boxes, for any references and any covering assignment.

## Upper bound and scale

For the construction's specified assignment, use one box per quartet with its first direct-edge endpoint fixed at 1, all else unrestricted, and a final box in which those $t$ endpoints all avoid 1. These boxes cover the grid. In the former box choose the reference with just its pinned endpoint changed from zero. The ordinary seed rows cover every nonzero old point and are eligible. At old zero the chosen quartet alone covers the box, since its zero tuple is excluded. On that quartet every compatible row has at most one disagreement: this includes its direct edge since one endpoint is pinned to 1. On the final box take reference zero. Every direct edge is inactive and all other rows are eligible, including the terminal. Hence $t+1$ boxes suffice.

The prime number theorem gives $t\asymp x/\log x$ and $k=1+B(P)+\sum_p(p-2)+5t\asymp x^2/\log x$, where the sum is over the fresh primes. The seed ordinary mass is $\Phi(P)+O(1)= (1+o(1))\log x$ from the ordinary harmonic estimates. The fresh unary mass is at most $\sum_p 2/p=O(1/\log x)$, and the pair mass is $O(t/x^2)$. Their contribution to the new ideal period mass is $\sum_p H_{p-1}/p=O(1)$. This proves all the assertions. The substantive feature here is the lower bound on descriptions of *any* assignment; the building block and its irreducibility are from the [previous cycle construction](cycles.md).
