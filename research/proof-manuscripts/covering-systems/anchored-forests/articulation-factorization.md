# Removing a branch that meets a unique-hole system at one digit

Codex (OpenAI), November 27, 2026. AI-authored written supplement to the [reciprocal component theorem](component-surplus.md).

Call a finite indexed family of cylinder supports *rigid* if the minimum number of holes over all independent assignments of forbidden tuples is one, and the minimum after any single row deletion is at least two. Repeated supports are allowed. Appending a movable full-grid singleton then gives irreducibility with respect to all residue reassignments. We work on the independent digit grid, so permutations of labels at a digit preserve the family of allowed assignments.

Suppose the rows are split into $t\ge2$ branches on coordinate sets $\{v\}\cup W_i$ with disjoint $W_i$. The shared variable $v$ has $p$ labels. Rows testing only $v$ may be assigned to any branch, once per row. In branch $i$ let $a_i$ be the minimum, over its own assignments, of the number of values at $v$ having any surviving continuation. Then the union is rigid **if and only if**
$$
                   \sum_{i=1}^t(p-a_i)=p-1
$$
and every branch becomes rigid after appending exactly $a_i-1$ independently movable virtual unary rows at $v$. The equality implies $a_i\ge1$. This characterizes both existence of a singleton placement and indispensability under every reassignment for arbitrary branch interiors.

**Necessity.** For fixed branch assignments write $U_i$ for the feasible root labels. A global root label is feasible exactly when it lies in $\bigcap_i U_i$, and the continuation counts there multiply. The fewest feasible root labels under arbitrary assignments is
$$
                 \max\{0,p-\sum_i(p-a_i)\}.
$$
The lower bound is the union bound on complements. For equality take minimizing assignments and permute the labels in each branch independently. Complement sets of the given sizes can be made disjoint when their sizes sum to at most $p$, and can otherwise be arranged to cover all $p$ labels (place consecutive intervals cyclically). Global rigidity requires a feasible label always and exactly one sometimes. This proves the equality above. In a global singleton witness every branch must therefore attain $a_i$, its forbidden root labels must be disjoint from the other branches' forbidden labels, and the common feasible label must have a unique completion in every branch.

For an intact branch any $a_i-1$ unary rows leave at least one feasible label. In the witness they can eliminate all but the uniquely continuing label, so the augmented minimum is one. Deleting a virtual row leaves at least two feasible labels. To handle an original deletion, suppose the augmented branch then had zero or one surviving tuple under some assignment. Without the virtuals that assignment has at most $a_i$ feasible labels. If it has fewer than $a_i$, the other branches can exclude them all by relabeling their witness complements, whose total size is $a_i-1$. If it has exactly $a_i$, the virtuals must leave a feasible label with a unique continuation. Relabel the other branches' witness complements to partition the other $a_i-1$ feasible labels of this branch. Independently send their uniquely continuing witness label to the one kept value. Each such permutation exists since the distinguished value is outside the forbidden set. There is then a global singleton after an original deletion. Both outcomes contradict rigidity.

**Sufficiency.** Every branch always has at least $a_i$ feasible root labels. Each augmented singleton witness has precisely $a_i$ before the virtuals are applied, including a label with a unique continuation. Relabel to partition all but one root label among the branch complements and to send all distinguished continuations to the remaining label. This attains a global singleton, and no reassignment can eliminate all root labels by the displayed sum rule. After deleting any original row in branch $i$, each assignment there has either more than $a_i$ feasible root labels, or exactly $a_i$ with at least two continuations at *each* one. Otherwise the $a_i-1$ virtuals can eliminate all tuples or leave only one, contrary to augmented rigidity. In the former case the intersection of feasible root sets now has at least two labels. In the latter, each feasible common label gives at least two global tuples. This proves full rigidity.

**Application to the extracted prime grid.** Use the graph connecting any two digits tested in the same internal support of a single [mutual-dependence component](component-surplus.md). Every internal support of size at least two is a clique. The complete bipartite graph on the two prime sets of the common-cofactor theorem is vertex-biconnected once each side has size two. It lies in one maximal biconnected block. Every row contributing one of its cross pairs has its *entire internal support* in that block, since a clique containing an edge of the block belongs to the same block. Each attached component outside a biconnected block can meet it at only one vertex; two attachments would give a path extending the block. At an attachment apply the theorem with the branches on the two sides. Retain the block side with its virtual unaries, and repeat until the attached components have all been removed. This produces a rigid local list on the biconnected block alone, retaining the full indexed grid rows and adding only unary constraints. Disconnected parts may first be dropped since their survivor counts multiply.

Thus the common cofactor grid is present even in an autonomous local instance which still withstands every row deletion. The statement concerns the projected, indexed constraints. Distinct integer moduli can project to the same support, and the virtual unaries have no assigned integer reciprocal mass. The original [surplus concentration](component-concentration.md) is for the whole mutual-dependence component; no further concentration of *net arithmetic surplus* on this biconnected block is assumed.

**Prior work and checks.** The [earlier rooted forests](anchored.pdf) use feasible digits at a parent, and [joined cycles](joined-cycles.md) give a sufficient gluing rule for particular cyclic pieces. The equivalence above permits general branch families. The finite calculation directly enumerates the possible continuation-count vector for every branch assignment, computes the minimum after every deletion from the global scalar products, and compares with the virtual-unary criterion. All 3,675 pairs of branch lists agreed, including 74 rigid unions. [Reproducible check](articulation-check.py)

For a separator consisting of two digits, arbitrary permutations of all ordered pairs need not be available: allowed relabelings at the two digits act independently. Counts of feasible separator tuples alone consequently do not justify the disjoint-complement argument. The shape of those sets and the locations of unique continuations are the next obstruction.
