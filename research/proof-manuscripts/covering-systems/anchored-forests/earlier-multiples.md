# Distinct earlier multiples at both endpoints of the common-cofactor graph

Codex (OpenAI). Written strengthening of the [separated-prime theorem](separated-prime-layers.md) for Erdős #1189.

Suppose $S-\Phi(L)\ge\epsilon\log k$ for an irreducible full-period set of size $k$, and fix **any** covering assignment. Under the hypotheses of the earlier theorem the fixed-cofactor graphs can be chosen with the following additional properties, for constants $\delta,\theta,\tau>0$ depending only on $\epsilon$.

1. In each selected row $pqd\le k$, the primes $p<q$ are the two largest internal primes of its component in that row. They lie in two separated ranges with $p>k^\delta$, $q>p^{1+\theta}$. In every selected row $p d>q k^\tau$. Thus the larger prime's cofactor exceeds even that prime by a polynomial factor.
2. Take the **union** of all retained graphs, across all their cofactor values, including a common graph for the logarithmically many simultaneous cofactors. Each vertex prime $v$ has its own list of at least $\lfloor v k^\tau\rfloor$ *other distinct input moduli*. Every modulus on its list is strictly smaller than the smallest retained graph modulus incident with $v$. Each is divisible by $v$, uses that prime as one of its two largest internal primes, and has its internal support in the **same block**, with its pair in the **same two prime ranges**. All these lists are mutually disjoint and avoid every retained graph row.
3. One may first erase any lists of at most $\lfloor v k^\tau\rfloor$ input rows at each first digit $v>k^\delta$ in the component, provided every listed row tests that digit internally. The new graph rows and their supporting lists can all avoid these erasures. The original component (with net surplus) can be chosen before the erasures. The block, ranges, graphs and lists may depend on them.

There is also an ambient bipartite multigraph in that block on those two prime ranges, containing the graph rows and all their supporting lists. Each of its vertices $v$ has degree at least $\lfloor v k^\tau\rfloor$, counting **distinct input moduli** as edges. This minimum holds on both sides at once, including the other endpoints of the supporting rows. The distinguished simple graphs additionally have the connectivity and cofactor conclusions of the earlier theorem. Paying for an endpoint twice using the same modulus would lose part 2.

**Proof.** Choose a component with $s_C-\phi_C\ge\nu\log k$ by [component concentration](component-concentration.md). Work only with its rows. Drop the terminal if present, rows $n>k$, rows containing a square of a prime $>k^\delta$, and rows whose small-prime part exceeds $k^\alpha$, where $\alpha=\sqrt\delta$. The [component estimates](component-surplus.md) bound these losses by $O(1)+O((\delta/\alpha+o(1))\log k)$. Rows with no internal large prime cost at most $(2\alpha+o(1))\log k$. Call the remaining rows with at least two internal large primes mixed. Label each by the two largest, $p<q$. Rows with exactly one large internal prime $v$ are set aside in its account. There are at most $v k^{2\alpha}$ such rows at $v$, by local support capacity and the bound on the small-prime part. The phrase “large” throughout means greater than $k^\delta$.

Root the component's block-cut tree. Drop mixed pairs with an old endpoint in their edge block, at mass cost $O(H_k M k^{-\delta})$, where $M=2+\log(1/\delta)$. All remaining pairs have both endpoints introduced there. Drop pairs $q\le p^{1+s}$ at cost at most $H_k M(\log(1+s)+o(1))$. These estimates use at most $H_k$ cofactor sum per pair. Split the exponent range $[\delta,1]$ into fixed bins of width less than $s\delta/8$. Group the mixed rows by introduction block and ordered bin pair. Each vertex belongs to only one introduction block, so it participates in at most a constant $J(\delta,s)$ of these groups.

Put $N_v=\lfloor vk^\tau\rfloor$ with $\tau=\alpha$. Put each prescribed erased row that survives the preliminary drops in the account of a large vertex that requested it. Rows already in a one-prime account need only be counted once. In each group of unerased mixed rows take one multigraph edge per input modulus. As long as a vertex has degree less than $N_v$, remove it with its incident edges, charging those rows to its account. Each vertex is peeled at most once per group. This leaves a multigraph of the claimed minimum degree.

In each remaining group, scan edges in strictly increasing order of integer modulus. Give each endpoint a separate bin of $N_v$ empty slots in this group. Put the edge in one free endpoint slot whenever possible, breaking ties arbitrarily. Keep an edge unassigned only if both endpoints already have all their slots occupied. Previously slotted edges never move. These slotted rows are also charged to their respective owners' accounts.

All account entries at $v$ are distinct multiples of $v$. There are at most $vk^{2\alpha}+(1+2J)N_v$ of them, including all prescribed, peeled and slotted rows. The sum of their reciprocals is at most
$$
          \frac{H_{v-1}+2\alpha\log k+O_{\delta,s}(1)}v .
$$
Summing the first terms uses only the first-digit mass in $\phi_C$; the others cost at most $2\alpha M\log k+O_{\delta,s}(M)$. Choose $\delta$ sufficiently small in terms of $\nu$ and then $s>0$ sufficiently small. All costs beyond $\phi_C$ total less than $(\nu/2)\log k$ eventually. Thus the unassigned mixed edges have reciprocal mass at least $(\nu/2)\log k$. This is why the erasures, peeling and assignment can be combined even though the erased rows alone may use a substantial share of the ideal mass.

For each endpoint of any unassigned edge, the occupied slots in its group give the asserted strictly earlier, disjoint list. In particular for $n=pqd$ there are $N_q$ strictly smaller multiples of $q$, forcing $pd\ge N_q+1>qk^\tau$. All rows in its own group are in the same introduction block, since their internal supports contain the pair edge. All peeling happened before the scan, giving the ambient minimum-degree conclusion.

Finally apply the block-mass and bin pigeonhole arguments of the [earlier theorem](separated-prime-layers.md) solely to the unassigned edges. They already lie in separated bins and have $d>k^\tau$. The partition into these bins incurred only a constant number of accounts per vertex. Within the chosen group the fixed-cofactor layer extraction and the weighted expansion proof apply unchanged. The occupied lists remain valid for every subcollection of unassigned edges in this group, simultaneously. This proves all assertions.

**What is missing.** The smaller multiples are genuine rows that pass the numerical capacity test at each endpoint. A further reassignment argument would have to make some of them cover enough of the same boundary fibres to free a row. Neither their number nor their disjoint ownership provides the required compatibility of the residues at the other coordinates.
