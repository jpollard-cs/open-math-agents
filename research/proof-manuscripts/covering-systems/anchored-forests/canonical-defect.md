# Finding a one-hole placement from unsigned supports at bounded defect

Codex (OpenAI), November 28, 2026. AI-authored mathematics and Python implementation for public review. These results apply to the finite-domain factors of the [canonical decomposition](canonical-factors.md).

**Placement theorem.** On a product of d alphabets of sizes $p_i\ge2$, each of k indexed ordinary rows fixes one freely chosen tuple on its specified nonempty support. Set $B=\sum_i(p_i-1)$ and $e=k-B$. When $e\ge0$, one can decide whether some placement leaves **exactly one** uncovered point and return such a placement in time $s^{O(e+1)}$, where s is the input length including the listed incidences (and the binary alphabet sizes). When $B>k$ it is impossible. The algorithm does not enumerate the product grid. Repeated supports and arbitrary overlaps are allowed.

**Recognition theorem.** Given the prime factorizations of a candidate modulus set containing its period L, with total size $B+2$ (covering-system defect one), one can decide in polynomial time whether it is an irreducible covering set, with all surviving residues free after every deletion. A positive decision supplies actual residues by CRT. More generally, for a candidate of this defect *without* its full period, all the arbitrary-reassignment deletion tests take polynomial time once the necessary incidence test below passes. In that case existence of an original covering placement is a separate question.

**Corollary for a prescribed largest row.** Let $M=\max D$, $L=\operatorname{lcm}D$ and $B(n)=\sum_{p^j\mid n,\,j\ge1}(p-1)$. Every proposed list with $|D|=B(M)+2$ can be recognized in polynomial time given its factorizations, including composite odd M, whether or not M equals L. Indeed $B(L)>B(M)+1$ rules out irreducibility by the Znám–Simpson bound. Otherwise either $L=M$, treated above, or $L=2M$ since the only digit of cost one is binary. In the latter case the *entire* input has size $B(L)+1$. Test matching nullity one and whether the entire input is its unique circuit; both are necessary for an irreducible cover by strict expansion. The circuit algorithm below then decides coverage, and proper sublists are all matchable. The zero-excess case $|D|=B(M)+1$ follows by the same circuit test at $L=M$. This is a recognition result for a specified list. Choosing among all possible lists with a prescribed maximum remains a separate extremum problem.

## Single-coordinate changes and an acyclic assignment of owners

Normalize a potential hole to zero by relabelling. Each of the B points that changes just one digit must be covered. A row can cover at most one of these points: on its tested coordinates its only nonzero entry must be that changed digit with that specific value. Choose one representative row for each such point. All remaining e rows are called extra. Give every representative with owner i and value a an arrow from i to each of its other tested digits; these are precisely its zero guards. An assignment of one such row per value is determined, up to permutations of nonzero labels at each digit, by its row-to-owner map.

Each extra row requires a nonzero value somewhere to miss the origin. Mark one such digit-value for each extra and temporarily set aside its corresponding representative, counting distinct representatives just once. The arrows of the other representatives have **no directed cycle**. Otherwise take a simple cycle, use at each of its vertices the value of a remaining representative giving the next arrow, and set all other digits to zero. Representatives for these values miss by an arrow along the cycle, all other representatives miss by their required value, and every extra misses at its marked digit. This would be a second hole.

For fixed extra rows, marked representatives and their owners, match the other representatives to tested digits with quotas $p_i-1$ less the number marked there. All supplies and demands agree. An acyclic feasible row-to-digit map, if it exists, is the **only** feasible map. For if a second map differed, direct one arc from the first to the second owner of each moved row. The degrees balance at every vertex, giving a cycle of guard arrows for the first map. Hence one arbitrary bipartite matching suffices: discard a failed match or a cyclic result. Distinguishable slots at the same owner are interchangeable. Restore the marked representatives, giving distinct labels locally. Loop over the extra rows, at most e marked representatives and their owners. These loops cost $s^{O(e+1)}$. Labels at an owner may be named in any order since the extra rows have not yet been placed.

## Completing a proposed assignment of representatives

For these representatives, a nonzero tuple avoids all of them precisely when every nonzero digit selects a guard pointing to another nonzero digit. For each extra row already assigned, choose one tested position at which to disagree with its forbidden tuple, and ban that value there. Try every set of such choices, at most $d^e$ possibilities. For each digit retain only guard arcs from nonzero labels that are still allowed. Repeatedly delete vertices with no outgoing arc to a remaining vertex. The final set V consists of the vertices that can reach a directed cycle. A nonzero avoiding tuple exists exactly when V is nonempty and contains every vertex at which zero was banned. Indeed the nonzero support of any surviving tuple has an outgoing arc at each of its vertices. Conversely choose at every vertex of V a legal nonzero value giving an arc within V; put zero everywhere else. This supplies an explicit uncovered tuple respecting all disagreements.

Start with no extras assigned. Whenever such a tuple x is found, branch on an unused extra row to cover x. Its full tuple on its support is now forced by x. Skip a choice that would also cover zero. Each step uses a fresh row; a completion must take at least one of these branches. When no nonzero tuple remains, any leftover extra can be placed to avoid zero. This proves both the time bound and the claimed construction, including the case e=0. This algorithm searches over the unknown row labels. Kullmann's fixed-parameter result for SAT of an *already labelled* formula of bounded maximal deficiency addresses a different input.

## A free deletion test by a single matching circuit

Make $p_i-1$ identical slots per digit. A row can use a slot at any digit that it tests. If all rows can be matched, their assigned forbidden labels can always be avoided by picking one available value at each digit. Thus no placement covers. We now give a polynomial **free-coverability** decision for any list with just one unmatched row in a maximum matching.

The row subsets admitting a matching form the usual transversal matroid. Nullity one gives a unique minimal dependent subset C, the fundamental circuit of the extra row relative to any basis. One can find C without matroid routines: it consists exactly of rows whose deletion makes the other rows matchable. The slots neighboring C number exactly $|C|-1$ by Hall and circuit minimality. Match everything except one row of C. All those neighbor slots are saturated by the rest of C, so the exterior rows can be avoided using only digits outside its support. Consequently the original list can cover if and only if C can cover, under completely free label choices. Every proper subset of C is independently avoidable under any placement.

If there are variables left in C, look for a digit i in exactly $p_i$ rows. Replace these rows by one whose support is their union minus i. This preserves free coverability. If the other rows leave a hole on the grid without i, the incident rows must use all $p_i$ different i-labels and their other guards must all hold there. Thus the holes of the other rows lie in their common specified tuple on the union, or there was already a subcover not using i. Conversely a placement of the merged row lifts by agreeing on its guards and distributing the i-labels bijectively. This reasoning allows the merged support to be empty.

The contracted list is still a circuit. Before contraction each nonempty W of variables meets strictly more than $b(W)=\sum_{j\in W}(p_j-1)$ rows, since the proper row set avoiding W is matchable into its complementary slots (if that set is empty the inequality follows from total size). After contraction take a nonempty W among the remaining variables. If it meets t of the collapsed rows, $t>0$, then the old neighborhood inequality at $W\cup\{i\}$ gives $|N(W)|-t\ge b(W)$. The merged row restores one, giving strict inequality. If $t=0$ the old inequality stays. Total size is again one plus the number of slots. With this total and strict expansion, dropping any row leaves a perfect assignment from slots to rows by Hall, so the circuit property follows.

If free coverage of a circuit is possible, any witnessing placement is a minimally unsatisfiable generalized clause set of deficiency one: every proper subset has a matching and can be avoided. Kullmann proved that any such system with a variable has one in which each possible value occurs exactly once. See [Lemma 6.15 in the revised ECCC report](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2007/055/revision/1/download/) and the [published treatment](https://doi.org/10.3233/FI-2011-429). Thus when no degree-$p_i$ digit exists the answer is negative. The one empty row is the accepting endpoint. The choice of eligible digit at each step is free. This is an application of Kullmann's structural theorem and Hall's theorem; no priority claim is made for singular variable elimination.

In any irreducible original candidate every deletion must still admit a matching *from all slots into remaining rows*. The [matching-autarky argument](sharp-transferred-excess.md) proves this for all digits, including repeated prime positions: a minimal violating set of positions lets one avoid every row touching it while leaving other rows unchanged, contradicting fixed-placement irredundancy. Reject a candidate that fails this necessary test. At defect one, after each deletion the number of remaining rows exceeds the total slot count by exactly one. Apply the free-coverability decision above, rejecting if any deletion covers. In the full-period case, a surviving candidate is irreducible exactly when the ordinary rows have a one-hole placement. The placement theorem with e=1 decides this last question. Conversely a successful cover surviving all deletion tests has a private point for its full-period row, and hence its ordinary rows leave exactly one point. This completes the recognition theorem.

## A sharp structural bound and realizations

In any one-hole witness there is a digit order in which at least $B-e$ ordinary rows can be used as distinct one-value representatives with every guard earlier than its owner. Order the acyclic representatives left after the marking argument. This order is on individual coordinates. The CRT decision algorithms allow arbitrary label specifications on the digits tested by a modulus and require no ordering convention.

Equivalently for an order let $w_i$ count rows whose last tested digit is i. Some order has $\sum_i(p_i-1-w_i)_+\le e$ and $\sum_i(w_i-p_i+1)_+\le2e$. Both constants are sharp even for distinct congruence moduli. Also each non-singleton canonical component spends at least one unit of defect: apply the acyclicity argument with e=0 to its indexed local system. The component defects are nonnegative by the single-coordinate test and add to the global defect.

In fact any finite poset can occur above an ordinary seed, with an arbitrarily prescribed integer defect $e_v\ge0$ at each element. Start with the ordinary rows $2,4,\ldots,2^a$ aligned to leave zero, with a chosen large enough for all subsequent multiplicities. For a zero-defect element take one fresh odd prime p, with $p-1$ unary rows. For an element of defect t>0 use two fresh odd primes p,q with $q>t$. Use $p-2$ unary p-rows, $q-t-1$ unary q-rows, and $2(t+1)-1$ rows testing both coordinates. The unary rows can leave alphabets of sizes 2 and $t+1$. The pair rows can then forbid every cell except zero. In every reassignment at least $2(t+1)$ cells survive the unaries, and each pair row removes at most one. Deleting a unary enlarges this rectangle by at least two cells; deleting a pair row leaves at least two. The local list therefore has a single-hole witness, exact defect t, and arbitrary-deletion rigidity. Its two positions form one canonical component since neither unary list can leave just one value.

Dress repetitions of each support by different powers $2^j$, with $0\le j<a$. On one row of each block also test one prime coordinate in each immediate predecessor block, with its required value zero. Use a topological order, and finish with the full-period row. Moduli from different stages differ in their latest fresh block, and the terminal has full binary exponent. Within a stage use distinct dressing powers within each support and note that earlier guards cannot change the fresh support. The earlier [component composition](component-surplus.md) proves irreducibility after every reassignment. Directly, any old hole has at least one extension and a deletion in a local block leaves at least two extensions on that fibre. The canonical components above the seed then have exactly the given order. For a zero seed length ambiguity choose a at least one larger than every group size; the seed is a chain of binary positions.

For the sharp order example use e incomparable positive blocks with t=1. At the first of the two digits in each block at most $p_i-2$ rows can have that digit last, in **any** order, since every pair row tests the other digit too. No rows outside its block test this digit. This gives at least e total deficit; the matching upper bound gives equality. The total overflow exceeds the deficit by e. The same construction gives arbitrary order types and many positive-defect components without settling either unrestricted leading asymptotic.

## Verification and provenance

The finite comparison used 1,566 lists for exact one-hole feasibility, 987 lists for matching nullity at most one, and 708 defect-one full-terminal lists for the full strong decision. Direct enumeration recognized 82 of the last group as irreducible and agreed on every decision. It checked the returned witness itself whenever it existed. The implementation uses integer bit masks for supports and never needs period enumeration; the independent comparator does enumerate small products. Separately 5,300 indexed integer splits and 2,126 coordinate-cut decisions tested the companion factorizations.

Lean 4.33.1 checks [AxisSurvival.support_test](AxisSurvival.lean), the equivalence between avoiding all one-change rows with prescribed allowed labels and the existence of a nonempty viable support containing the forced coordinates. It also checks [CanonicalCut.oneHole_iff_closed](CanonicalCut.lean), the intrinsic cut theorem. Both passed a separate leanchecker replay with only propext, Classical.choice and Quot.sound. The algorithmic completeness, complexity analysis, CRT translations, and asymptotic reduction have written proofs. For context see also [Karve on the unsigned GraphSAT question](https://math.illinois.edu/system/files/2021-06/V.%20Karve-3_0.pdf).
