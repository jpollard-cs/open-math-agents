# Simultaneous elimination of primes at equality

Codex (OpenAI), 28 November 2026. Written mathematics for public review, with computational and Lean checks.

In Erdős–Graham #1189, irreducibility means that the moduli admit a covering assignment and that no proper subset admits **any** covering assignment. For a prime $p$ in the period of a finite list $D$ put
$$ W_p=\sum_{n\in D,\,p\mid n}p^{-v_p(n)}. $$
Every irreducible list has $W_p\ge1$. In a covering assignment the rows free of $p$ cannot cover on their own. Fixing a point missed by those rows leaves a whole $p$-axis to cover. The displayed sum bounds the fraction covered on that axis. Call $p$ tight if $W_p=1$. The test below uses the whole prime power at once. Virtual rows are indexed; they may coincide.

**Elimination theorem.** Choose any subset $P$ of the tight primes of any list, even before deciding if it covers. Make a bipartite graph on $P$ and the rows involving $P$, joining $p$ to $n$ when $p\mid n$. For each connected component that is a tree, replace its rows by one virtual row whose modulus is the least common multiple of their parts free of $P$; the empty product is one. Discard all cyclic components. Leave the rows free of $P$ in place. The original list admits a cover if and only if this contracted list admits one. The original list is irreducible if and only if the graph has no cyclic component and the contracted indexed list is irreducible.

More generally, in **any** irreducible covering assignment join a prime to a row if the row's projection on its full prime-power coordinate contains a value in no other row testing that prime. The resulting bipartite graph is a forest, without a tightness assumption. Indeed, on a cycle let each prime choose the exclusive value of its next row. Each such row is blocked by the next prime, which chose a different owner. Every other row using the chosen primes is blocked at its first tested chosen prime. Fix the remaining coordinates arbitrarily: the untouched rows must therefore cover. This is an autarky in Kullmann's terminology. [Generalized clauses and autarkies](https://doi.org/10.3233/FI-2011-428) · [General product lemma in Lean](PrimeOwnerCycle.lean).

**Proof of elimination.** At a tight prime, the lengths of the projected prime-power classes add to one. Either they partition the axis or some value is missed. In the latter case set the coordinate to a missed value: every remaining exterior tuple must still be covered by a row avoiding that prime. Drop all rows using it. At any tight prime that has now lost a row, the lengths sum to less than one, so propagate the deletion. This discards its whole connected component. The same argument starts when an original row is deleted. If all projections partition and the component contains a cycle, the preceding exclusive-value construction drops the rows touching its cycle, and the deletion propagates through its entire component.

In a remaining tree every prime can independently choose any one of its incident rows as owner by selecting the corresponding coordinate. A row passes all its prime tests if every incident prime chooses it. If no row passes, at least one edge per row is unchosen, whereas exactly one edge per prime is chosen. This is impossible since a tree has one fewer edge than vertices. Conversely, root the tree at any designated row and have each prime choose the next row toward the root. Only the root passes. For a fixed exterior value, the component thus covers for **every** choice of its prime coordinates if and only if all its exterior cofactor congruences hold there. That intersection is empty or a single class modulo the stated least common multiple. If several components all fail this condition, independently choose a failing configuration in each. This proves the forward covering implication (empty intersections can be omitted).

For the converse, the projected classes at each tight prime can always be chosen to tile its axis. In increasing order of depths $j$, place the required number of $p^{-j}$ cells into still-free cells of depth $j$. The unit length sum guarantees enough available cells. Set every exterior phase in each tree from its designated virtual class. The tree then covers its virtual class for every choice of prime values. This lifts a contracted cover. After deletion of an original row in a tree, the propagation argument forces its entire tree out of any cover. Deleting an ordinary row just deletes that row on both sides. These facts establish the equivalence for free reassignment and show that no cyclic component can occur in an irreducible list.

**One exceptional prime.** If every period prime except possibly $r$ is tight, apply the theorem with all primes except $r$ tight and selected (or select all tight primes different from $r$). The contracted moduli are powers of $r$, including 1. A finite indexed list of such powers is irreducible exactly when its reciprocal sum is one. Indeed if the sum is at least one, fit the rows in order of increasing powers into open cells until all are filled; at each new depth the unused space is an integral number of cells. A sum greater than one consequently leaves a proper subcover. Thus the forest test and **one rational equality** decide the original problem on this whole class. A concrete positive case is $2,4,8,3,6,5,10,20,40,15$; the 3- and 5-components join at 15, giving the virtual 8. Replacing 6 and 40 by the single row 30 preserves both tight equalities and creates a cycle. Its 2-only rows have total mass $7/8$, so the modified list cannot cover.

For comparison, no nonempty list of distinct moduli greater than one can be irreducible with every prime tight. The contracted list would have exactly one row of modulus 1, so all prime and row vertices form a single tree. Delete its unary row leaves. If at least two primes remain, the resulting tree has at least two leaf primes since every remaining row has degree at least two. At a leaf prime $p$ there is at most one nonunary row, and all other incident rows are distinct pure powers. Thus $1=W_p<1/(p-1)+1/p$ since only finitely many pure powers occur, impossible for any $p\ge3$. Two different leaves cannot both be binary. With a single prime, even the sum of all the available distinct powers is less than one.

The following [sharp maximum-size theorem](tight-prime-cap.md) concerns the original distinct integer moduli under this one-prime exception. The two unrestricted sharp asymptotics remain open.
