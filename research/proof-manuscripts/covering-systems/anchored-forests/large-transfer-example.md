# A prime-group projection certificate with a random-access constructor

Codex (OpenAI), November 28, 2026. Finite application of the [sharp excess theorem](sharp-transferred-excess.md).

There is an explicit irreducible cover of **9,914,735,791 distinct moduli**, largest **250,009,500,090**, with the following property. Use cutoff 12,000. Even after reserving any one row and setting aside any further quarter of each recipient prime's rows, at least **84 complete batches** each see at least $95^{84}$ distinct cofactor residues. This holds for every allowed partition of the remaining rows and every assignment to the complementary rows in the batch's dyadic bin. All 28 recipient primes are between 48,017 and 48,341 and therefore belong to the same bin. This is a construction and verification of its properties. No minimum or record in that cardinality is asserted.

[The Python constructor](large-transfer-example.py) returns the modulus and aligned residue at any zero-based row index with the option `--row INDEX`. Its default run checks the arithmetic data without materializing nearly ten billion rows or their period.

Use $z=500009$, $a=3915$, and as donors all 897 primes $3915<r\le12000$. Retain $\lceil100r/306\rceil$ ordinary rows at r and distribute the other $r-1-\lceil100r/306\rceil$ evenly among the first 28 primes above 48,000, carrying the remainder cyclically. This gives 4,749,043 donated rows, between 94 and 289 per donor–recipient pair. These deliberately rational retention counts specify the example independently of floating-point asymptotic parameters. The old base consists of all prime powers at most z with base at most a. Each recipient gets the least old divisors as its guards. Later stages and the terminal agree with the general theorem.

The program checks all old guard counts, special caps, stage radices and both extreme omission counts. For each q the number of complete batches is four even after the allowed omissions. There are fewer than $q-1$ original q-owned rows outside them. One q label can avoid all those rows and the terminal guard, just as in the proof. Over any surviving prefix, each r offers at least 95 choices. Since the entire q group contains just $q-1$ ordinary recipient-stage rows, **at most one** batch can contain fewer than $q/2$ donor rows. In each of the other three batches these rows test at least $\lceil q/(2\cdot289)\rceil\ge84$ different donors. This gives $3\cdot28=84$ batches and the stated count. Stage distinctness, coverage and arbitrary-reassignment irreducibility follow from the general surviving-child and terminal proof. The finite certificate checks the construction's arithmetic premises; it does not enumerate the full period or formally verify the general theorem in Lean.
