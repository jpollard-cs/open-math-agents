# Complete prime-product boxes with every row indispensable

Codex, at the request of Jordan Pollard. AI-authored written proof for review, 27 November 2026.

A complete grid of products can occur in a genuine irreducible covering set, even together with its complementary rows and even when all residues can be reassigned after deletion. In fact grids can be placed simultaneously in every dyadic scale triple in the ranges below, within an arbitrarily small relative error of the grouped leading cap and an additive error tending to zero of the zero-defect reciprocal optimum. The mechanism is that one right-hand prime can rule out exactly one vector of surviving left-hand digits. This gives an exact threshold and an explicit constructor.

## The saturated case on a complete bipartite graph

Use distinct left primes p_i (1 ≤ i ≤ h) and fresh, mutually distinct right primes q_j (1 ≤ j ≤ s). An indexed family may temporarily use repeated internal moduli. Fix 2 ≤ b_i ≤ p_i and positive integers t_ij. Put a_j = Σ_i t_ij and assume a_j ≤ q_j. Use the following rows:

* p_i − b_i unary rows of modulus p_i for every i;
* q_j − a_j unary rows of modulus q_j for every j;
* t_ij rows of modulus p_i q_j for every pair.

**The family can cover if and only if s ≥ P = ∏_i b_i. When s = P every index is indispensable under arbitrary reassignment.** This includes cases in which some unary multiplicities are zero.

Under arbitrary assignments the unary rows leave at least b_i left values and a_j right values. Fix a surviving vector of left values. A right digit can be avoided unless all its surviving values are hit by active incident pair rows. There are just a_j incident rows, each covering one right value when its left condition is met. Thus to rule out a left vector at j, every incident row must be active. Since each left coordinate occurs, j can rule out at most one vector. If no j rules it out, independently choose an uncovered right value at each j. CRT then gives an ordinary integer hole. At least P right vertices are necessary. If one left unary row is deleted, the product of the lower bounds increases strictly. If one right unary or one pair row is deleted, its right vertex cannot rule out any left vector. This proves all deletion assertions.

For sufficiency, use distinct unary residues at every vertex and index P of the right vertices by the remaining left vectors. At j prescribe that vector on the left of every incident row, and assign bijectively the a_j surviving right values among those rows. Any surviving left vector then rules out a whole right digit. This supplies an actual cover.

At equality the size of the indexed family is Σ_i(p_i−b_i) + Σ_j q_j. The pair multiplicities cancel the decrease in right unary counts exactly. Once all these primes occur its local defect (size minus one minus the sum of p−1 over the supported primes) is P−1−Σ_i(b_i−1). Consequently merely increasing the multiplicity of an indispensable box need not increase the number of rows beyond the prime-digit budget.

## Distinct moduli and simultaneous scales

Here is a quantitative separation between the presence of many boxes and a gain in reciprocal mass. For a parameter z tending to infinity consider every dyadic interval [u,2u) contained in [z^(1/4),z^(1/3)], every [w,2w) contained in [z^(2/3),z^(3/4)] and every [v,2v) contained in [z^(1/2),z^(3/5)]. For every triple of these intervals, one and the same irreducible covering set contains **all** the distinct products U·W·B for suitable sets in the three respective intervals. Here U,W consist of primes, |U| = h = floor((log₂ z)/2), |W| = 2^h, and |B| = floor(z^(1/4)). The logarithm in log₂ z in this paragraph is to base two. The primes can be disjoint between all boxes. Each b in B has only core prime factors. Every box product has largest prime q in W, and its cofactor p b is at least q. All are exceptional rows in the earlier reciprocal-sum terminology.

The resulting set has k ∼ F(z) = Σ_{p≤z}(p−1), largest modulus M ∼ k log k and reciprocal sum S = R₀(k)+o(1). These are constructed sizes, with no assertion here for every exact k. There are order (log z)³ disjoint logarithmic scale triples. The reciprocal mass of all the planted products together tends to zero.

**Core.** Choose all left and right sets disjointly first. The prime number theorem supplies more than enough primes in each dyadic interval: the demand per interval is O((log z)² 2^h) on the right and O((log z)² h) on the left. The sum θ of reciprocals of all these removed primes is O((log z)⁴ z^(−1/4) + (log z)³ z^(−1/6)). Let y = (1−1/√log z)z. The base uses all other primes r ≤ y, with their full powers through z, in increasing prime order. At a position r^e take r−1 distinct side moduli r^e d and one distinguished row carrying the full period. Keep every eligible d<r and fill the remaining slots with eligible d≤min(2r,z). Eligible means d divides the preceding part of the period with primes strictly smaller than r.

For r below the first omitted prime, all d<r are available. For larger r, with T=min(2r,z), at most Tθ candidates through T contain an omitted prime, and at most 2π(T) contain a prime ≥r, since T≤2r. All other candidates divide the preceding core. For r≤z/2 this leaves more than r choices, uniformly after the first omission. For z/2<r≤y the excluded count is O(z/log z)+zθ, smaller than z−y. The construction therefore works. Its permanent side rows are distinct and at most z² (higher positions have r≤√z). Distinctness follows from their largest prime and position. Its distinguished modulus is D, the full core period. The temporary indexed core is irreducible by the one-digit refinement proof.

**Planting.** In each box use the saturated family above with b_i=2 and t_ij=t=floor(z^(1/4)). All q_j exceed h t. Choose t distinct core divisors from [v,2v) for B. Almost all integers in that interval qualify: the excluded prime multiples number O(vθ + number of omitted primes ≤2v). More directly, up to 2v the union bound is at most 2vθ, which is o(v). All primes above y exceed 2v and all prime powers dividing an integer through 2v are within the baseline exponent cap. Dress the t pair rows at each i,j by B to obtain the full box.

Replace the distinguished core row successively with these packets on fresh primes. For clarity the old distinguished row d is initially D and can thereafter be taken to equal D times a single left prime from the preceding packet. For every ordinary unary group, dress its indices with distinct divisors of D of size at most z. These are plentiful: the number of integers up to z excluded by missing primes is at most zθ plus the number of primes between y and z, which is o(z). Leave one left unary index available as the distinguished row in each nonfinal packet. Dress that one with D. Include the preceding tagged prime, if any, as a dressing on another unary row so that the exterior lcm includes it. Include each maximal core prime power as a dressing somewhere among the right unary slots. There are at most z such powers, all ≤z, while the right unary slots per packet number at least 2^h(z^(2/3)−ht) ≫ z. Assign each required power once and fill the rest with distinct allowed divisors within each unary group. When the preceding tagged prime is used it is outside D and divides d. The exterior lcm is exactly d, including at the final packet, where every row is permanent.

For the freely reassigned converse, over each hole left by the old ordinary rows the active internal indices must themselves cover the independent fresh coordinate product. Irreducibility of the indexed packet forces every index active there. Hence all old holes satisfy every exterior residue constraint, and lie in their intersection, one class modulo d. Contraction gives a covering reassignment of the old surviving indices and forces them all to be present. Conversely, align the exterior residues with the old distinguished class and combine with the internal cover by CRT. This is the earlier [coprime substitution](../sparse-labels/) applied to the new packets. New permanent moduli are distinct: within a group the dressings differ, different groups have different supports on that packet's prime set, and every new row has at least one prime omitted from all earlier periods.

**Size and weight.** The old core contributes (1+o(1))F(z) permanent rows: the removed primes and the terminal interval (y,z] account for o(F(z)), and all higher positions cost O(z log z). The packets together contribute at most O((log z)³ (h z^(1/3)+2^h z^(3/4))) = o(F(z)). The largest permanent unary packet rows are at most z·z^(3/4), and pair products are at most z^(1/3+3/4+3/5), both o(z²). There exists a surviving ordinary prime r∼z by the prime number theorem (the planted primes lie below z^(3/4)). Its r−1 distinct side cofactors force maximum at least r(r−1). This yields M∼z²∼k log k.

Relative to the baseline reciprocal sum U_z=Σ_{p^j≤z} H_{p−1}/p^j, omission of the entire rows at the planted primes costs O(θ log z)=o(1). The first-position mass on (y,z] tends to zero by partial summation of the prime number theorem, and the higher-position tail tends to zero by absolute convergence. Among the retained ideal cofactors d<r the missing d have a planted prime divisor. Their reciprocal mass at any position is at most θ(1+log r) before division by r^e. Summing gives O(θ log z)=o(1), since Σ_{r≤z}(1+log r)/(r−1)=O(log z) over primes. Replacements contribute at most the omitted mass since they are ≥r. Thus the core contributes U_z+o(1). Every unary packet group at prime p has reciprocal mass at most (1+log p)/p using the distinct dressings; this costs O(θ log z)=o(1) in total. The box products cost at most Σ_boxes (Σ_U 1/p)(Σ_W 1/q)(Σ_B 1/b), which tends to zero even if the last factor is replaced by 1+log z. The previous [exact-size zero-defect reciprocal estimate](../reciprocal-sums/) gives R₀(k)=U_z+o(1) for k∼F(z).

The [earlier extraction theorem](../sparse-labels/) produces dense dyadic triples from a hypothetical gain in the unrestricted reciprocal coefficient. This construction shows that simultaneously indispensable complete boxes, polynomially large factors, and many disjoint scale triples alone are possible at the baseline coefficient. The unresolved step is a lower or upper bound on their aggregate reciprocal mass under the same essentiality requirement.

The [overfilled extension](overfilled-stars.md) allows excess pair rows at each right prime. Its rigidity formula gives a limit on the right primes that a single refined row can carry.

## Verification

The finite example in [saturated_boxes.py](saturated_boxes.py) uses left primes 3 and 5, right primes 7, 11, 13 and 17, two pair rows per edge, and dressings 32 and 64. Its 64 distinct moduli have maximum 69,632. All 255,255 internal residues are checked, so the cover holds throughout the integer period 1,045,524,480. A second enumeration checks the possible sets of killed left vectors for 33,552 right-star assignments, including reassignments after every type of deletion. In the finite enumeration, assigning outside a residual domain cannot enlarge the killed set, so such choices may be ignored for upper bounds. This example has different primes and modulus set from the earlier 64-modulus one-edge construction. [SaturatedBox.lean](SaturatedBox.lean) separately proves that a saturated right fibre kills at most one left assignment and the resulting finite counting obstruction. The multi-box theorem above is a written proof for review; the entire integer example in this supplement has no end-to-end Lean certificate here.
