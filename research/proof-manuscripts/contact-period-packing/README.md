# When a period joins a polyomino to itself

Written by Codex (AI), at Jordan Pollard's request, 27 November 2026. This is a written proof with reproducible independent finite checks. Priority and external review are unconfirmed.

## The exact fixed-period packing problem

Let P be a finite edge-connected set of s cells in the square grid, with fixed orientation and integer translations. Fix a nonzero integer vector v. Assume the copies P+nv, n in Z, are pairwise disjoint as cell sets and P shares a full cell edge with P+v. Consider **all** packings whose centers are invariant under translation by v. The transverse arrangement may be arbitrary, including aperiodic arrangements. Density here may be taken to be upper Banach covered density in grid squares.

Write v=d p with d positive and p primitive. Choose an integer vector u with det(p,u)=1. A point has unique integer coordinates i p + j u. Form the finite set F of differences P-P in these coordinates, reducing the first coordinate modulo d. For j>0 let I(j) be the residues i modulo d such that (i,j) is absent from F. Put g=min{j>0 : I(j) is nonempty}. If W is the difference between the maximum and minimum det(p,x) on P, then g≤W+1. Everything in this computation is integer arithmetic.

**Theorem.** The exact best density is s/(d g). For each i in I(g), the lattice generated by v and i p+g u attains it. More generally a list of rows in increasing transverse order is a packing exactly when every consecutive displacement (i,j) has i in I(j). No two distinct rows in a packing have the same transverse coordinate. Thus every doubly periodic maximizer has constant transverse gap g, and any sequence of successive phases from I(g) produces a maximizing packing. Sparse defects can also leave an upper asymptotic density unchanged.

For example take the five cells (0,0),(0,1),(1,1),(1,2),(2,1) and v=(3,0). The row spans three horizontal lines. The least gap is two, at which phases 0 and 2 modulo 3 both work. One may choose either phase at each new row independently. The exact density under this period is 5/6.

## Proof

Use the embedded square-grid graph on cell centers, with a segment when two occupied cells share an edge. Two disjoint cell sets have disjoint graphs, since square grid edges with distinct endpoints do not cross. Project the graph of the row P+Zv to the cylinder R²/Zv. Call its image R. It is compact and connected. A path from a vertex x to x+v exists by the edge contact and connectedness. Its image is a closed walk of winding one. Splitting at repeated vertices gives simple cycles, at least one of nonzero winding. A loop edge or a two-edge cycle is allowed in the quotient. Fix one essential embedded circle J in R.

Such a circle separates the cylinder into two components, one containing each end. This follows from the Jordan curve theorem by adjoining the two ends as points on a sphere: a simple curve with both ends on the same side would bound a disk avoiding both ends. The upper components of disjoint essential circles are nested according to their order. An essential circle above another has its entire upper component above the lower circle.

Suppose rows R and R+w are disjoint. The circles J and J+w are disjoint and strictly ordered. This comparison persists under translation by w. If J+w is above J, the sequence J+kw for k≥0 is strictly increasing by nesting, and analogously if it is below. Zero transverse displacement is impossible, because for an integer displacement with det(p,w)=0 some positive multiple is zero on the cylinder. If det(p,w)>0, the circles escape to the upper end, so J+w must be the upper circle. The connected set R is below J+w and R+w is above J: each avoids the other circle and contains its own.

Now take any ordered sequence with consecutive disjoint rows. The respective copies of J are ordered by the preceding argument. Each row is above the preceding circle and below the following circle whenever these exist. For any two nonconsecutive rows an intervening circle therefore separates their **whole graphs**. This proves sufficiency of consecutive comparisons, including finite or one-sided sequences. For two rows, disjointness of the graphs is equivalent to disjointness of the cells, which in turn is exactly the test (i,j) outside F.

Every packing has at most 1+floor((H-1)/g) rows in any H consecutive transverse levels. In d consecutive primitive longitudinal coordinates exactly one center belongs to each such row. For an upper Banach density argument, partition the integer plane into d by H parallelograms in the (p,u) basis. For fixed H, boxes meeting the boundary of a large grid square contribute O(H-dependent constant times side length) points. Let the side length tend to infinity, then H tend to infinity. This bounds center density by 1/(dg), uniformly under translations of the window. Passing from centers to covered cells multiplies density by s, up to a boundary error, because P is finite and the packing disjoint. Repeating a least allowed displacement gives a genuine lattice packing by the circle argument, and its determinant is dg. An arbitrary doubly periodic arrangement with equality has all its gaps equal to g by averaging one finite cycle. Conversely, with every gap equal to g the row count differs from H/g by at most one, regardless of phases. This proves all assertions.

## Consequences for arbitrary finite period groups

Suppose a packing with k motifs per full-rank period lattice L beats every one-copy lattice packing. No copy can share an edge with its translate by any nonzero v in L. Otherwise the preceding theorem, applied to that period, produces a lattice at least as dense. In particular, with fewer than k empty cells per period, even one such contact forces a lattice **tiling**: an integer lattice packing of an s-cell shape at density above s/(s+1) has determinant s.

This removes a difficulty when searching for shapes by their images in a finite abelian quotient. Let A be the cell image in G=Z²/L. Build its **induced labeled grid graph**, retaining the oriented unit step of every edge, including loops and parallel edges. Connectivity is necessary. Starting at zero, propagate integer cell coordinates along a spanning tree. If all edges agree with the resulting coordinates, *every* connected lift equals this one up to translation: integration along any path in the induced graph gives the same result, and each connected lift supplies such paths. If a discrepancy occurs, *every* connected lift has some self-contact period: for that lift, if every induced edge were realized by its unit step then there would be no discrepancy around any closed walk. An unrealized edge joins a cell to a unit neighbor contained in a nonzero L-translate of the shape. Thus all connected lifts in the inconsistent case are covered by the theorem. This saves an exponential spanning-tree search. The argument works at every index and every number of copies.

## Four copies and three holes

**Finite theorem.** Every edge-connected polyomino with at most 31 cells that packs four integer translates per lattice with exactly three empty cells in the period admits a lattice tiling. The period lattice may be oblique. This includes all connected lifts, and shapes with holes are allowed.

For s cells the index must be 4s+3. Enumerate all finite abelian groups C_a × C_b of this order with a dividing b. These are exactly the possible abstract quotients of the integer plane (Smith normal form). For each group enumerate sets B of four centers up to affine automorphism. Anchor a center at zero and enumerate the remaining triples. Automorphisms are obtained by testing all generator images respecting their orders for surjectivity. For each B find every cell set A with A+B a direct sum missing exactly three elements, anchoring one missing element at zero by translating A. At a remaining unassigned site either mark a hole if the budget permits, or place any disjoint translate of B containing it. This exhaustive branching treats the four centers as the translated pattern and the cells as its starting positions. Deduplicate the resulting sets A up to affine automorphism; their tiling test depends only on A and the grid step images.

Finally enumerate all generating pairs of step images, up to negating either and interchanging them, since these operations give grid isometries. A zero step would force a connected lift to be a straight segment, since no edge in that direction can occur in an injective lift. For primes dividing a, generation means the two step vectors are independent modulo the prime; for primes dividing b but not a, generation means their second coordinates do not both vanish. Check the induced graph and apply the preceding result for inconsistent lifts. For a consistent lift directly enumerate the lattices of determinant s in Hermite normal form. Injectivity modulo any of them exhibits a tiling. As an extra check the resulting spanning-tree shape in each inconsistent case is tested the same way.

The complete enumeration found 9,984 center motifs over 35 group types, 2,602 cell-set orbits counted across those groups, 26,459 inconsistent and 31,339 consistent connected step-pair cases. Every tested lift had a determinant-s tiling. The computer proves only the stated finite range. An independent Python check starts from all connected grid shapes up to eight cells and directly excludes all four-copy, three-hole packings for each shape lacking a lattice tiling. It also tests the fixed-period optimizer and small exact independent-set counts on finite cylinder segments.

## Reproduce and continue

Compile and run the C++20 census with `g++ -O3 -std=c++20 census.cpp -o census && ./census`. The output should match `census.txt`. Run `python3 verify.py` for the separate small tests in `independent.txt`. The small checker uses no nonstandard dependencies.

The original question asks whether some polyomino requires more than two translates per lattice for maximal packing density. The fixed-period theorem and the four-copy census leave this open. A three-hole four-copy example above size 31 remains possible, as does a counterexample with a lower density and a different motif. Random growth at prescribed densities, including elongated shapes and local mutation to eliminate competing lattices, yielded no verified example. A promising next search can discard every inconsistent lift immediately and use centered cell-set symmetry or parallelogram centers to reduce exact covering searches at larger orders.
