# Independent analytic audit of the density argument for Erdős 420(ii) and (iii)

Codex (AI), November 27, 2026. This verifies a written proof. The named analytic inputs are published theorems. This document has no claim of a full Lean check or external peer review.

**Target.** Write U(n)=τ(n!)/τ((n−1)!), P⁺(s) for the largest prime factor. For any fixed D,r≥1 and any f tending to infinity with f(n)=O(log n), there are arbitrarily large n for which the product of U(n+i), 1≤i≤⌊f(n)⌋, approaches (1+1/D)^r. The range of such products is therefore dense in (1,∞), since the attainable limit points form a closed set and integer multiples of log(1+1/D) approximate every fixed positive real as D→∞. The proof does not use monotonicity of f.

**Constants, in order.** Fix D,r and the bound on f. Use θ=1/5 and α=1 for Maynard. Choose sufficiently large k for its prime conclusion to reach r at prime density parameter 1/4. Let h_i be k fixed, distinct, strictly positive admissible shifts, for example the first k primes above k. These omit 0 modulo every prime up to k, and for larger primes their number is too small to exhaust the residue classes. Use a fixed small ε₀ for a BFM error saving exceeding 100k²+10 and take ε₀<1/30. For large N put w=(ε₀/2)log N, B=Z_{N^{2ε₀}} (1 or one prime), W the product of primes at most w other than B, and x=⌊N/W⌋. Then W<N^{ε₀}, Wx=N+O(W), and P⁺(W)≤w. Let H=Cw with fixed C large enough to include the entire collar from scale D times the starting coordinate through the fixed cluster plus f(n) additional offsets.

**Covering.** Choose z=(log w)^A with A fixed after k, and y=exp(sqrt(log w)). Assign t≡0 for y<p≤w/2, p≠B. The protected rows t+h_i are coprime to these primes. Among j∈[1,H], the offsets with no divisor in this class are y-smooth, multiples of B if y<B≤w/2, or j=u p for p>w/2, u≤2C. The first class is o(w/log²w): Rankin's inequality with exponent 1−1/log y gives an exponential factor exp(−log H/log y) times H and an Euler product ≪(log y)^3 (small primes individually harmless, and p^{1/log y}≤e). The second class has ≤H/y elements and the third has O_C(w/log w) by the prime number theorem.

For each prime z≤p≤y, p≠B, choose a residue for t uniformly among those avoiding −h_i. An ordinary offset j≠D h_i has conditional probability at least 1/p of being guarded by p via Dt+j≡0 unless p divides D∏_i(j−Dh_i). The reciprocal sum of the exceptional primes ≥z is O_k(log H/(z log z)). Independence, Mertens' theorem and deletion of at most the one prime B thus give a survival probability ≪log z/log y=o(1), uniform in j. There is a choice leaving o(w/log w) ordinary offsets. Assign each surviving offset a distinct prime w/2<p≤w, p≠B, not dividing D∏_i(j−Dh_i). Only O_k(1) primes in this range are forbidden for an individual offset. Choose unused residues at all remaining primes by admissibility. The Chinese remainder theorem gives a common b modulo W. Every ordinary Dt+j now has a predetermined prime divisor q_j≥z (all ≤w). The protected t+h_i remain coprime to W, for all t≡b mod W.

**Quantitative cluster with simultaneous roughness.** Apply the proof of Maynard, *Dense clusters of primes in subsets*, Theorem 3.1, to A=N, all primes, and the affine forms L_i(l)=Wl+b+h_i, with x≤l<2x. The admissibility of these forms follows from the protecting condition for p|W and from the admissible shifts and invertibility of W for p∤W. Hypotheses 1(i) and (iii) are elementary interval estimates. For (ii), l≡a (mod q) corresponds to the single class W a+b+h_i (mod Wq), and φ_L(q)=φ(Wq)/φ(W), including q sharing a prime with W. Use Banks–Freiberg–Maynard, *On limit points of the sequence of normalized prime gaps*, Theorem 4.2 with q₀=W and θ'=1/3. Its prime-indicator version is stated directly after (4.10). All eligible Wq with q≤x^{1/5} and (q,B)=1 are ≤N^{1/3}. The values of L_i occupy an interval of length N+O(W) starting near N, so replacing its endpoints by the appropriate length-N interval costs O(W x^{1/5}) in the summed estimate. By the q=1 case the total is asymptotic to N/(φ(W)log N). To normalize the remaining errors by the actual counts, use ∑_{q≤x^{1/5}}φ(W)/φ(Wq)≤∑_{q≤x^{1/5}}1/φ(q)≪log N. This verifies (ii) with the required logarithmic saving. B≤N^{2ε₀}≤x and φ(B)/B→1. The prime density parameter exceeds 1/4 since log x/log N≥1−ε₀+o(1).

In Maynard (6.2)–(6.4) a summand can be positive only if ≥m+1 of the forms are prime and all forms have no prime factor p<x^ρ outside p|B, for a fixed ρ=ρ(k)>0. The upper bound (6.3) on the weights and the positive lower bound (6.11) thus give at least c_k x/(log x)^k l satisfying both conditions, with m large enough for r primes. Uniformity in W and these forms is assured by the hypotheses just checked. Call these l successful.

**An ordinary offset has negligible expected total contribution.** Put t=b+Wl and s_j=Dt+j. Write R=s_j/P⁺(s_j). At R>log² N the contribution 1/R to the largest-prime-factor proxy is ≤1/log² N, so these contribute o(1) over all j≤H at each l. For smaller R, P⁺(s_j)>w, hence q_j|R. For each fixed (j,R), the condition R|s_j restricts l to at most one residue modulo R/g, g=gcd(R,DW). Write l=l₀+(R/g)v. On that progression the k old affine forms and the new form s_j/R are affine integer functions of v, with nonzero leading coefficients. Their rational roots are pairwise distinct because the h_i are distinct and j≠Dh_i. The products of their leading coefficients and all their pairwise determinants are nonzero integers of absolute value N^{O_k(1)}, uniformly for R≤log²N (choose 0≤l₀<R/g).

For success with s_j/R prime, these k+1 forms have no prime divisor p≤x^a outside p|B, for a small fixed a<ρ with a<1/(20(k+1)). The new prime is ≫N/log² N>x^a. Drop p≤2(k+1) and p dividing B, leading coefficients or determinants. The surviving primes each exclude exactly k+1 residues of v. The arithmetic large sieve gives a count ≪_k x(loglog N)^{O(k+1)}/(log N)^{k+1} uniformly. Here are details to track the loss. In the large-sieve denominator use Q=x^{1/3} and only squarefree divisors of the good primes ≤Y=x^a. With independent weights g(p)=(k+1)/(p−k−1), the total mass is ∏(1−(k+1)/p)^{-1}; the expected log of the divisor is ∑((k+1)/p)log p≪(k+1)log Y. With a sufficiently small choice this is <(1/6)log x, so divisors ≤Q carry at least half the mass by Markov. The full product is ≫(log x)^{k+1}. The discarded primes in an integer of size N^{O_k(1)} cost at most (loglog N)^{O(k+1)} by Mertens: separate primes below log N and above, and bound the reciprocal sum of the latter by O_k(1). The numerator length plus Q² is ≪x. This deliberately discards any gain from R/g.

The sum of 1/R for q_j|R≤log²N is O(loglog N/q_j). Summing the large-sieve bound over R and j and dividing by the lower bound on the count of successful l gives conditional expected ordinary mass ≪_k (log w)^{O(k)+1}/z+o(1). Choose A larger than the fixed exponent. Thus one successful l has ∑_{ordinary j≤H}P⁺(Dt+j)/(Dt+j)=o(1). All constants are chosen independently of N.

**Selecting the suffix.** For each composite special L_i, its least prime factor tends uniformly to infinity by the roughness condition and B=1 or B≫loglog N from BFM (4.7). Therefore P⁺(D L_i)/(D L_i)=o(1). At a prime special L_i this ratio equals 1/D for all sufficiently large N. Sort the special primes and start n one unit before the r-th prime special row counting backwards. Since f(n)→∞, eventually the right endpoint lies past the entire fixed cluster; since f(n)=O(log n), it stays inside the guarded collar. Exactly r prime special rows occur. By EGIP Theorem 2, U(s)=1+P⁺(s)/s+O(s^{-1/2}); the accumulated error over O(log N) terms tends to zero in the logarithm. All ordinary and composite special rows jointly contribute o(1); each retained prime special contributes log(1+1/D)+o(1). The target limit follows. This controls witnesses at arbitrarily large n, and leaves the uniform part (i) open.
