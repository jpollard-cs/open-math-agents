# Independent audit of one-step, rational and irrational factorial slopes

Codex (AI), at Jordan Pollard's request. Written check, 27 November 2026.

Let u(m)=log(τ(m!)/τ((m−1)!)), a_d=log(1+1/d), and κ=Σ_{d≥1}a_d/d. Erdős, Graham, Ivić and Pomerance's Theorem 2 gives u(m)=a_d+O(m^{-1/2}) where d=m/P⁺(m). Each u(m) is nonnegative and bounded. On a dyadic interval, the total proxy mass over d>D is O(X/(D log X)+√X): terms with P⁺(m)≤√(O(X)) contribute O(√X), and the other cofactors d≤√(O(X)) contribute at most Σ_{d>D} O(X/(d² log X)) by π(O(X)/d)≪X/(d log X).

## Moving endpoints

For integer nondecreasing n↦h(n) between 1 and n^(1−ε), analyze the lag s≤min(h(X),C log X). At starts with h(n+s)=h(n) the difference in log ratios consists of an incoming strip of s rows and a disjoint outgoing strip of s rows. Integrated over all starts, the number of further incoming rows due to changes of length is at most s h(3X), by telescoping, and their log contributions are bounded. If discarding all starts crossed by a change of h, the two ordinary strips contribute at most O(s² h(3X)) on this exceptional set. Both errors are o(sX/log X). For each fixed row offset the map n↦n+h(n)+j is strictly increasing, with at most O(h(3X)) holes or endpoint discrepancies relative to the usual dyadic interval. This proves the single-mark intensities and the large-cofactor tail for the moving endpoints.

For two fixed cofactor marks, partition into the intervals where h stays constant. There are O(h(3X)) such intervals; all shorter than X^{ε/2} have total length O(X^{1−ε/2}). On each long interval, place the two required affine prime forms in the simultaneous divisibility progression. Outside the finitely many primes dividing either cofactor or 2, a prime dividing the offset difference reduces the two distinct forbidden residues to one. Thus the local upper-sieve penalty is the single factor J(g)=∏_{p|g,p>2}(p−1)/(p−2). Uniformly for g+j of size O(X) and nonzero, the sum of these penalties for j in any s consecutive positions is O(s+loglog X). Primes above log X cost a bounded factor. Expanding the remaining Euler product gives a main density times s bounded by a convergent product, plus the sum of all coefficients ∏_{2<p≤log X}(1+1/(p−2))=O(loglog X). In a block's two-pair sum this yields O(s(s+loglog X)) penalties. On starts with marked multiplicity M≥2 the total M is bounded by 2 binom(M,2), which controls the scaled first absolute moment as well as test functions.

It follows that the first-order signed jump locations are ±a_d, each of mass 1/d, for s=o(log X) after scaling by log X/s, and their first absolute moment is 2κ. For s=⌊δ log X⌋, δ fixed small and h(X)≥s, either signed direction has ≫δ X starts of size at least (log 2)/2. Indeed for each direction retain prime marks, bound competing bounded marks by the pair estimate, choose a large fixed tail cutoff and then small δ; Markov controls the omitted mass. Negative jumps are enough to preclude concentration about *any nondecreasing* center; positives handle any nonincreasing center, irrespective of the magnitude of center increments. For an arbitrary asymptotically slow center the same follows from either direction. This is multiplicative concentration for the unlogged ratios when log deviations are used.

## Rational and irrational slopes

For β>1, put m=n+1; a new row t enters when β(m−1)<t≤βm. Outgoing denominator mark is u(m). At coprime β=A/B, proportional rows occur exactly when Am=Bt, so m=Bz and t=Az. If fixed small cofactor e occurs in z=e p, the net mark is a_{Ae}−a_{Be}, with intensity 1/(Be) after scaling by log X. The remaining entering marks have weights β/d when A does not divide d, and the remaining outgoing ones have weights 1/d when B does not divide d. Each arriving t occurs exactly once over the whole sample interval. With finite cutoffs take B E outgoing and A E incoming; this keeps the paired marks intact. Other coincidences have two nonproportional fixed linear prime forms after splitting modulo B, hence zero first-order mass by the upper sieve. At irrational β an outgoing mark m=d p and an entering mark t=e q would force floor(βd p)−j=e q for one of finitely many bounded j. For fixed product L of primes ℓ≤z with ℓ∤e, Vinogradov's prime equidistribution for irrational βd/L makes floor(βd p) uniform modulo L. Each ℓ rules out one residue; taking X to infinity and then z to infinity gives o(X/log X) simultaneous occurrences. Two distinct arrivals lie at a bounded nonzero integer difference and admit the same ordinary two-form upper sieve. The resulting generic law has only +a_d of mass β/d and −a_d of mass 1/d.

Since each daily log increment is bounded in terms of β, all absolute moments of order ≥1 can be passed through the cofactor tail bound. In particular the signed first moment is (β−1)κ; the first absolute moment for rationals is lower than the generic (β+1)κ by (2/B)Σ_e a_{Ae}/e.

Any β>1 has ratio liminf ≥1/2; the unpaired outgoing d=1 atom saturates this at all nonintegers. At integer A≥2 the mandatory row Am benefits from P⁺(Am)≥P⁺(m), so pairing it with m gives at least (1+v/A)/(1+v)+o(1) where v=P⁺(m)/m≤1. All other arrivals contribute factors at least one. This proves the lower bound (A+1)/(2A), attained as a limit by the e=1 net atom. For β=(B+1)/B, the limsup is also exact at 2: only m divisible by B produces two arrivals, and then the aligned ratio at (B+1)z,Bz is ≤1+o(1). A single unpaired incoming d=1 atom supplies the lower bound.

At a microscopic detuning β_X=A/B+c/X with fixed c, the exact alignment at m=Bz lies in the entering block iff c≥0 and c(m−1)/X<A/B. Thus the same-step paired fraction θ(c) is 0 if c<0, 1 if c=0, and max(0,min(1,(A/B)/c−1)) if c>0. Unaligned pairs reduce to bounded nonzero Am−Bt and to the usual sieve. The resulting measure is the mixture of the rational and generic measures with weights θ(c), 1−θ(c). The rational at c=0 has an exact one-sided jump in this crossover.
